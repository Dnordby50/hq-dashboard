# PRESCOTT EPOXY SALES PLAYBOOK

**Version 3.0 | July 2026 | Supersedes all prior sales documents**

This document replaces: Total Sales Training, PEC Sales SOP, Sales Process / Sales Map, PEC-SALES-004, and all per-rep scripts. Where anything conflicts with this document, this document wins.

## SECTION 1: WHY WE WIN AND WHY WE LOSE

Read this section first. Everything else in this playbook exists to serve it.

### We win because we solve problems

That is the whole thing. Everything else in this playbook is machinery for doing it.

Nobody wakes up wanting an epoxy floor. They want a problem to go away. Your job is to find that problem, say it back to them, and tell them exactly how it goes away. Do that and the price becomes the cost of a solution instead of the cost of a floor.

**The mechanism, in four moves:**

1.  **Find the problem.** It comes out in discovery. If you did not find one, you did not do discovery, and you have nothing to sell against.
2.  **Name it back to them.** "So the thing that's really bugging you is X." They need to hear that you heard it.
3.  **Answer it specifically.** Not a general pitch about our system. The specific answer to their specific problem.
4.  **Build value along the way.** Value is not a step. It goes in at every turn, in small pieces, from the moment you walk up. By the time you say a number, the value should already be there.

**Their problem, your answer:**

| What they tell you | What you say |
| :-: | :-: |
| "We need it done before the holidays / before we move in / before the party" | Tell them exactly when we can do it. Give a real date. Timing is not an obstacle for us, it is a selling point. One to two day install, back in service in 24 hours. |
| "This concrete is impossible to keep clean" | Tell them how easy it is to clean when we are done. Oil wipes up. Hose it, mop it, done. Bare concrete is a sponge. Coated concrete is a countertop. |
| "We had this done before and it peeled / the guy disappeared" | Tell them how we own our follow-through. A 10-year warranty in writing, a local office, a showroom they can walk into, and dedicated floor crews. The last guy is gone. We are still here, and we come back. |
| "The floor is dusting / spalling / falling apart" | The coating seals it and stops the dusting for good. |
| "It gets slick when the cars come in wet" | Full flake broadcast is a non-slip surface. That is one of the reasons we do it. |
| "It's just ugly, I'm embarrassed by it" | Before and after photos. This is a pride problem and it is the easiest one we solve. |
| "We're selling the house" | ROI and curb appeal. It photographs well for the listing and it is one of the cheapest square-foot upgrades in the house. |
| "It's my gym / workshop / where I work on cars" | Impact, chemical, and UV resistance. Dropped weights, spilled solvents, hot tires. Built for exactly that. |

That table is not a script to memorize. It is the pattern: whatever they said, come back to it with a specific answer. If they give you a problem that is not on this list, the move is the same.

**The 10-year warranty is your closer on anyone who has been burned.** It converts "what if this peels like the last one" into "then they come back and fix it." Garage and interior are 10 years. Exterior is 2. Say it right.

### We lose for three reasons

**1. Follow-up died.** One touch and gone. The lead went cold while a competitor stayed in front of them.

**2. Price, straight up.** They were shopping on price and we were never going to be the cheapest. Some of these are unwinnable. Most are winnable if value got built first.

**3. The rep never asked for the job.** Presented, answered questions, left a quote behind, waited for the customer to volunteer a yes. This is the most skipped step in sales and the most expensive.

### The three mistakes our previous reps made

Do not repeat these.

**Quoted before finding the WHY.** Walked in, measured, priced. No pain point discovered, so nothing to close against. The number lands on nothing and becomes the only thing the customer can evaluate.

**Discounted too fast.** Led with a promo instead of building value. This trains customers to negotiate and tells them the first number was not real.

**Let follow-up slide.** Logged the estimate in the system and moved on.

## SECTION 2: WHAT WE SELL AND WHAT WE PROMISE

### Company differentiators

  - In business since 2022, purpose-built as a dedicated epoxy company, not painters who added floors as a side service
  - Number one rated epoxy company in the area
  - Physical showroom customers can visit before committing
  - Multiple dedicated floor crews, not splitting focus between trades
  - Full mechanical grind and profile on every floor, no shortcuts on prep
  - 100% solids epoxy base coat
  - Full flake broadcast (non-slip, hides flaws)
  - High solids polyaspartic topcoat (UV, impact, and chemical resistance)
  - 15 to 20 mil system versus competitors at 3 to 5 mil
  - Return to service in 24 hours, installed in 1 to 2 days
  - Local office staff, which means accountability and service after the sale

### Warranty (state it exactly this way)

| Application | Warranty |
| :-: | :-: |
| Garage floors | 10 years |
| Interior floors | 10 years |
| Exterior surfaces | 2 years |

Do not say "10 year warranty" as a blanket statement on an exterior job. Exterior is 2 years. Overpromising the warranty is the fastest way to turn a won job into a loss.

### The showroom

Use it two ways:

1.  **As a credibility point** during the company story. "We have a showroom in town. Most epoxy companies work out of a truck."
2.  **As a close on a hesitant customer.** When someone is stalling on color, on trusting the finish, or on whether we are real, invite them in. A showroom visit is a strong buying signal and gives you a second touchpoint that a competitor does not get.

## SECTION 3: PRICING, DISCOUNTS, AND FINANCING

### How pricing works now

Pricing comes out of the **TopCoat estimator**. It is tiered by coating system, not by square footage bracket.

**Retired, do not use:**

  - The $6 per sq ft under 750 / $5 per sq ft at 750-plus flat rate. This is gone.
  - The $5.50 per sq ft promo. Retired. Do not offer it.

### Presenting the price

Present **one price plus add-ons**. We do not run a good/better/best option ladder.

Add-ons (crack repair, stem walls, moisture control, ramp and lip coating) are calculated by the TopCoat estimator. You do not need to memorize rates. Build the full scope in the estimator and present the complete number.

### Discount authority

Aaron can offer **10% off, flat, on any job, without approval.**

**A warning that matters more than the rule.** Discounting too fast is one of the three mistakes that cost us jobs before. Having authority is not permission to lead with it. The 10% exists for one purpose: to close a job that is genuinely stuck after value has been built and the objection has been isolated. If you find yourself offering it before the customer has objected, you are giving away margin you did not need to give.

Sequence: build value, present price, handle the objection, offer financing, and only then consider the discount.

### Financing

**Enhancify. Lead with it.**

Rates as low as 0% for approved customers. Introduce financing during the presentation, not as a last resort after they balk. Framing the job as a monthly number instead of a total makes a premium system accessible and keeps you from reaching for the discount.

Script: "Before I show you the number, I want you to know we offer financing through Enhancify with rates as low as 0% for approved customers. A lot of our customers use it to get the floor they actually want instead of the one that just fits this month."

### Deposit

Historically 50%. We are lowering friction at signing.

**Aaron's call, with a 25% floor.** Read the room. If a smaller deposit is what gets the signature today, take it. Never go below 25%.

## SECTION 4: SYSTEMS

| What | Where |
| :-: | :-: |
| Leads, estimates, jobs, invoices, follow-up queue | **TopCoat** |
| Job photos | **CompanyCam** |

TopCoat is the system of record for everything except photos. DripJobs is no longer used. Any older document referencing DripJobs is obsolete.

### What must be logged in TopCoat on every estimate

  - Square footage (laser measured)
  - Coating system and color selected
  - Add-ons quoted
  - Concrete condition (hard, soft, flaking, cracking, moisture signs)
  - Equipment accessibility
  - The customer's stated pain point and their WHY
  - Decision maker status (who needs to sign off)
  - Budget signal, if given
  - Competition status (are they getting other quotes)

The pain point field is not optional. It is what the follow-up cadence is built on. A follow-up call that references the customer's actual problem converts. A follow-up call that says "just checking in" does not.

## SECTION 5: THE SALES PROCESS

### Step 1: Office qualification (before the appointment exists)

The office owns this. No estimate gets scheduled without it.

**Qualifying questions:**

1.  "What kind of project are you looking to get done?"
2.  "What's prompting you to get this done now?" (their WHY, the most important answer on the call)
3.  "Have you had this done before, or is this your first time?"
4.  "Have we done any work for you previously?" (phone calls only, flag returning customers)
5.  "How did you hear about us?"
6.  "Do you have a rough budget in mind, or are you in the early stages of pricing?"
7.  "Are you getting a few different quotes, or are you looking to move forward fairly quickly?"
8.  "Who will be the decision maker when it's time to move forward, just you or your spouse as well?"
9.  "What's your ideal timeline?"

**Opening script:** "Thanks for reaching out to Prescott Epoxy. I'd love to get you set up with a free estimate. Before I do, I want to ask a few quick questions so we can make the most of your time when our specialist comes out. Is that okay?"

Everything goes in TopCoat. Flag any job where both decision makers need to be present at the estimate. That flag is worth more than any other note you can leave.

### Step 2: Prep and arrival

**Before you leave:**

  - Company brochures
  - Epoxy flake samples (full set)
  - Laser measurer, charged and tested
  - Tablet with TopCoat access
  - Pens, notepad, business cards
  - Branded polo or sweatshirt, clean pants, clean appearance

**Before each appointment:**

  - Review the TopCoat notes. Know their WHY before you knock.
  - Call or text 30 to 60 minutes ahead
  - Arrive 10 minutes early. If you're earlier than that, call or text before pulling in.
  - Park respectfully and visibly. Do not block the driveway.

### Step 3: The warm-up (first 2 to 3 minutes)

The customer is sizing you up. The goal is to get them to relax and see a professional, not a salesperson.

  - Slow your energy down. Do not rush in.
  - Comment on something genuine about the home or the space.
  - Avoid politics, religion, and complaining about business.
  - If they offer water or coffee, accept it. It lowers tension.
  - Ask them to call you by your first name.

**Script:** "Hey [Name], great to meet you. Thanks for having me out. I was just noticing [something genuine]. Before we get into everything, tell me a little about what you've got going on here."

### Step 4: Discovery (the most important step)

**This is where the job is won.** We win by solving problems, and this is where you find the problem. No problem found, nothing to sell against.

You are not selling in this step. You are hunting for the problem. Do not measure yet. Do not talk about the system yet. Do not think about the number.

Everything they say is either a problem, a constraint, or noise. Your job is to separate them and find the one thing that, if you solve it, makes the decision easy.

**Discovery questions:**

1.  "What's happening with your floor right now that's driving this?" (the problem)
2.  "What bugs you the most about it day to day?" (the problem, sharper)
3.  "What's the ideal outcome you're looking for when this is done?"
4.  "Have you had epoxy done before that failed?" (a yes here is a warranty close)
5.  "What's the main use of this space, vehicles, workshop, gym, storage?"
6.  "Are you worried about staining, durability, or appearance?"
7.  "Do you have a starting investment number in mind?"
8.  "Is there anything else I should know before I put together a recommendation?"
9.  "Who else will be involved in the decision, is your partner home today?"

**Then name the problem back to them.** This is the single highest-leverage sentence in the entire appointment:

"So just to make sure I'm understanding you correctly, the thing that's really bugging you is [the problem], you're concerned about [Y], and what you want when this is done is [Z]. Is that right?"

That sentence does more than any brochure. It proves you listened, and it gets them to confirm the problem out loud. Once they have confirmed it, everything you say afterward is an answer to something they already agreed matters.

**Then answer it, right there, before you measure.** Do not save the answer for the pricing conversation. If they said the concrete is impossible to keep clean, tell them how easy it will be to clean when we are done. If they said timing is tight, tell them when we can do it. If they said the last floor failed, tell them how we own our follow-through. Small pieces of value, dropped in as you go. By the time you present a number, you should not be building value, you should be collecting it.

### Step 5: Company story and credibility

Before you touch the tape measure, show them who you are. Pull out the tablet. Reviews, before and after photos, the warranty, the showroom.

**Script:** "Before I take any measurements, I want to show you a little about who we are. We've been the number one rated epoxy company in the area for the past couple years. We split off from our painting company because epoxy is a completely different trade and we wanted to be the best at it, not just add it on. We also have a showroom in town if you ever want to come see the finishes in person. Let me show you a few recent projects."

### Step 6: Measure and build

Now you measure. Narrate what you are doing so they stay engaged.

  - "I'm measuring this section because..."
  - Take CompanyCam photos: full garage overview, cracks and damage, access points, anything they specifically pointed out
  - Keep noting pain points as you walk
  - Build the full scope in the TopCoat estimator including add-ons

### Step 7: The pre-price build-up

**Never lead with the price.** By now the value should already be built, because you have been dropping it in since the warm-up. This step is where you collect it. Walk them back through their own problem and the answer you already gave them.

1.  Remind them of the problem they described
2.  Explain specifically how our system addresses that problem
3.  State the warranty (10 year garage and interior, 2 year exterior)
4.  Reference the reviews and reputation
5.  Introduce Enhancify financing
6.  Then present the price

**Script:** "Based on what you told me, you've had a floor peel before and you don't want that happening again. Here's what we do differently. We go 100% solids epoxy, full flake broadcast, and a polyaspartic topcoat. That's a 15 to 20 mil system where a lot of guys are putting down 3 to 5. And the part that actually matters to you: it's covered by our 10-year warranty in writing, we have a local office and a showroom, and we have our own dedicated floor crews. If something goes wrong, you know exactly who to call and we come back. We also offer financing through Enhancify with rates as low as 0% for approved customers, so this doesn't have to be one lump sum. Let me show you the numbers."

**Trial close, right after the price:**

  - "How does that sound?"
  - "Is this in line with what you were expecting?"
  - "I know you've probably seen a few other quotes. How does this stack up?"

### Step 8: Objections

Objections are requests for more information, not rejection.

**Isolate first.** Before handling anything, find out if price is the real objection:

"Besides the investment, is there anything about me, my company, or the system itself that would keep us from moving forward?"

**The 5-step framework:**

1.  Empathize: "I totally understand that."
2.  Clarify: "Can I ask, is it the overall price, or is there a specific part that doesn't feel right?"
3.  Confirm: "So if we could address [their concern], that would work for you?"
4.  Address: go back to their pain point and the value you built
5.  Ask again: "Given that, is this something we can move forward with?"

**Common objections:**

| They say | You say |
| :-: | :-: |
| "It's too expensive" | "I hear you. Can I ask, is it the total number or the monthly investment? We offer financing through Enhancify with rates as low as 0% for qualified customers, so you can get it done now and spread the payments out. And keep in mind the cheapest bid usually costs more in the long run when you're fixing it again in two years." |
| "I need to think about it" | "Absolutely, I respect that. Can I ask what specifically you need to think through? If there's something I haven't answered well, I'd love the chance to address it now." |
| "I'm getting other quotes" | "That makes sense. While you're comparing, make sure you're comparing the same scope and warranty. Ask what mil thickness they're putting down and ask to see the warranty in writing. A lot of guys are at 3 to 5 mil with nothing behind it. We're at 15 to 20 with 10 years in writing, and we'll still be here to honor it." |
| "My spouse isn't here" | "No problem at all. When's a good time for both of you to be available so I can walk through it together? I want to make sure all the questions get answered." (Better: catch this at qualification so it never happens.) |
| "I have to wait on funds" | "Understood, that happens. We offer financing through Enhancify with rates as low as 0% for approved customers. A lot of our customers use it to get the project done now without waiting. Would that change things?" |
| "My old floor peeled" | Not an objection. This is a buying signal. Lead with the 10-year warranty and how we own our follow-through. |

### Step 9: Close

**Residential: present the price and ask for the job on-site. Every time. No exceptions.**

You do not leave a residential appointment without either a signed contract or a scheduled follow-up with a specific date and time.

**Close questions:**

  - "Do you want to move forward?"
  - "Would you like to pay the deposit with a check, card, or would you like to run the financing application?"
  - "Now that you've seen what's involved, can you see why it's priced the way it is?"
  - "How long do you think it'll take to get the deposit together?"

If yes: go straight to paperwork. Do not celebrate. Get signatures while momentum is high.

**Conditional close if no:** "It sounds like you need a little more time to get things lined up. If you can get that sorted, would you move forward with us? Let's set a time to reconnect. What does your week look like?"

**Commercial: different process.** Discover and measure on-site, then send a formal written proposal and close on a follow-up. Do not force an on-site close on a commercial job.

### Step 10: Referrals and handoff

Ask for referrals right after signing, when excitement is highest.

"Thank you, we're confident you're going to love the result. Do you know anyone else who might be looking at a floor? We'd love the introduction, and we have a referral program."

**Sales to production handoff.** Before you leave, log everything production needs:

  - Preferred contact method
  - Every pain point and concern mentioned
  - Specific promises made (color, timeline, warranty coverage, special requests)
  - Access instructions, gate codes, parking, pets
  - Deposit collected and payment structure

Production's job is to exceed what was promised. They can only do that if they know what was promised.

## SECTION 6: FOLLOW-UP

### Ownership

**Office owns the lead until the estimate is scheduled. Once it's scheduled, Aaron owns it.**

After the estimate is run, follow-up is Aaron's, entirely, for 30 days. Not shared, not split, not automated away.

Follow-up dying is one of the three reasons we lose jobs. This is the fix.

### The 30-day cadence

| When | Action |
| :-: | :-: |
| Day 1 | Call. Leave a voicemail if no answer. |
| Day 3 | Call plus text |
| Week 1 | Call |
| Week 2 | Call plus email |
| Week 3 | Call |
| Week 4 | Final call. Bring new information if you have it: a schedule opening, a seasonal window, a new photo of a similar job. |

**Every touch must carry value.** Not "just checking in." Reference their specific pain point. Send a photo of a job like theirs. Send a review from someone in their neighborhood. Offer a showroom visit.

Urgency, used honestly: "We're booking out a couple weeks. I can hold a spot for you if that helps, no pressure."

After 30 days, the lead returns to the office automation.

### When Dylan calls

For jobs we really want, an owner call carries weight. Speak directly to the objection from the estimate and bring new information.

## SECTION 7: SELF-GENERATED LEADS

**Target: 1 to 2 per week. This is enforced, not aspirational.**

**How:**

  - Knock doors near active jobs and near estimates you just ran
  - Leave flyers and business cards
  - Drop yard signs at busy corners and past jobs
  - Canvass evenings and weekends

**Script:** "Hi, I'm Aaron with Prescott Epoxy. We're working right nearby. We do high-performance garage floor coatings, one to two day install, and a 10-year warranty. Thought I'd stop by in case you're considering it. Here's some info."

Log every self-gen lead in TopCoat and tag the source so it shows up in the weekly review.

## SECTION 8: KPIs AND ACCOUNTABILITY

### Targets

| Metric | Target |
| :-: | :-: |
| Close rate | **40%** |
| Self-generated leads | 1 to 2 per week |
| Estimates run | Track weekly |
| Average job size | Track weekly |
| Sales volume | Track weekly |

### Reading the close rate honestly

Aaron is at **39% year to date**, which is on target. Last month was **19%**, which is roughly half.

One month is a small sample and could be noise. It could also be the leading edge of a real problem. Do not average it away and do not panic on it. Diagnose it.

**When close rate drops, find the step that broke. In order of likelihood:**

1.  **Discovery got skipped.** Rushing to the measurement. Check TopCoat: are the pain point fields filled in on last month's estimates or are they blank?
2.  **The close was never asked for.** Check whether the estimates that died had a scheduled follow-up on the calendar or just a quote sitting in the system.
3.  **Follow-up died.** Count the touches on last month's lost estimates. If most died at one or two touches, that is the answer.
4.  **Lead quality shifted.** Check lead source mix. If a channel changed, the rep is not the problem.
5.  **Price resistance changed.** Look at whether the losses cluster at a job size or system tier.

Run that diagnosis before changing anything about the process.

### Weekly 1-on-1

Weekly one-on-one between Dylan and Aaron. Agenda:

1.  KPI review: close rate, estimates run, volume, average job size, self-gen count
2.  Wins from the field, what worked
3.  Losses from the field, which step broke on each
4.  One training topic (roleplay, article, ride-along debrief)
5.  Action plan if any KPI is off target

## SECTION 9: THE SHORT VERSION

If you remember nothing else from this playbook:

1.  **Find the problem, then solve it out loud.** No problem found means no discovery happened and nothing to sell against. Name it back to them, answer it specifically, and keep building value the whole way through.
2.  **Do not lead with a discount.** Build value, present price, isolate the objection, offer financing. The 10% is a last resort, not an opener.
3.  **Ask for the job.** Residential, on-site, every time.
4.  **Follow up six times over 30 days.** Every touch carries value. Never "just checking in."
5.  **Warranty: 10 year garage and interior, 2 year exterior.** Say it right.
